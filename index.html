Featured Research – PhD Thesis

A Psychological Study of the Impact of Cultural Barriers on Pastoral Counseling Effectiveness in Buguma City, Nigeria

By Ukwoma Obugheni Police | EIBCS2526P012 | June 2026
El-Elyon International Bible College – Submitted to El-Elyon Seminary

Study Area: 39 Polo (compounds) of Buguma City, Asari-Toru LGA, Rivers State – Kalabari cultural setting.

Method: Descriptive survey. Population of pastoral counselors and congregants in PFN and CAN churches. Sample 407 respondents (53.1% female, 46.9% male) using Taro Yamane formula. Instruments: structured questionnaire and interview guide. Frameworks: Multicultural Counseling Theory and African Psychology.

Summary of Findings

The study examined five objectives related to cultural barriers:

  • Cultural beliefs (including riverine water-deity worship and traditional worldview) were significantly associated with help-seeking preference for traditional approaches over pastoral counseling.
  • Stigma and respect for elders / fear of community disclosure reduced willingness to disclose personal issues to pastoral counselors.
  • Preference for tradition and communal decision-making influenced perception of effectiveness – counseling seen as less legitimate when it conflicts with established customs.
  • Language differences (Kalabari vs English) affected emotional expression – respondents reported easier disclosure of deep feelings in Kalabari heart language.
  • Overall impact: Cultural barriers had a statistically significant impact on perceived effectiveness of pastoral counseling. The study did NOT measure church dropout or clinical outcomes – it measured perceptions, preferences and help-seeking.

Recommendations (Brief)

Integrate Kalabari language use for emotional disclosure; train counselors in multicultural competence; involve respected elders as bridge-builders; provide confidential counseling spaces to address stigma; document indigenous coping alongside pastoral care.

When a person is in pain, the words they use matter. A church member may speak English at work and in church, yet find it hard to describe grief, fear or shame in English. In their heart language, the same person may speak freely.

This is one of the main themes of the PhD study behind our featured research, "A Psychological Study of the Impact of Cultural Barriers on Pastoral Counseling Effectiveness in Buguma City, Nigeria". This article shares what the study found and what churches can do with it. You can read the full summary on our [research page](/research.html).

## About the study
The study surveyed 407 pastors and adult church members across 51 PFN- and CAN-affiliated churches in the 39 Polo of Buguma City, Asari-Toru LGA, Rivers State. This is a Kalabari cultural setting. All 407 questionnaires were returned. The survey asked about four cultural barriers to pastoral counseling and about how effective counseling was perceived to be.

## What respondents said
- **Language and stigma stood out.** Respondents most strongly endorsed language differences (mean 4.12) and cultural stigma (mean 4.07) as barriers, on a five-point scale. Preference for tradition (3.63) and cultural beliefs and practices (3.21) followed.
- **Counseling was still rated effective.** The overall effectiveness rating was 4.15.
- **Respondents supported solutions.** Strategies to reduce the barriers received the highest agreement (4.24). The top strategy was training pastors in Kalabari language and culture (4.29).
- **Language links to depth of sharing.** The study cites earlier research (Wokoma and Briggs, 2024) linking counseling in English rather than the heart language, Kalabari, to a 38 percent reduction in depth of disclosure among non-fluent English speakers.

## What the study cannot say
Good research is honest about its limits. The study measured perceptions, not church attendance or clinical outcomes. The relationships it found were small, and two of the scales had low reliability, so findings on them are less secure. These results are a useful guide for Buguma City, not proof for every community.

## Five practical steps for churches
**1. Let people speak in their heart language.** When someone struggles to express a deep feeling, invite them to switch to the language they feel most at home in. If you do not speak it, involve a trusted, trained interpreter or counselor, and agree confidentiality first.

**2. Train counselors in the language and culture of the people they serve.** This was the top-rated strategy in the study. Even basic training helps a counselor to hear what is meant, not only what is said.

**3. Reduce stigma openly.** Teach, from the pulpit and in small groups, that seeking counseling is wisdom, not weak faith. People come when they believe they will not be judged.

**4. Protect privacy.** Offer a private room where conversations cannot be overheard, and explain clearly what stays confidential and what cannot (for example, serious risk of harm).

**5. Work with elders and professionals.** Involve respected elders as supportive partners, without letting them replace confidential counseling, and build links with clinical services for cases that need more than pastoral care.

## A note on care and referral
Pastoral counseling is valuable, but it does not replace professional mental health care. If someone may harm themselves or others, or has a serious mental illness, help them reach a qualified professional or emergency service quickly.

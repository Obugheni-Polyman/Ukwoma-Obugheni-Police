Deforestation and Rural Development in Ikwerre

A case study of Ikwerre Local Government Area, Rivers State, Nigeria

Ukwoma Obugheni Police | B.Sc. Geography and Environmental Management, University of Port Harcourt, March 2015
Supervisor: Dr. (Mrs.) I. A. Ogbole

Free summary

What this study is about

People in Ikwerre clear forest to earn a living, and the same clearing is thinning the forest that supports that living.

This study asked 200 household heads in ten Ikwerre communities how fast the forest is disappearing, what is driving it, who benefits and what it costs. Most said the loss is rapid. Chainsaw timber work, farming and wood fuel production are the main ways people use forest land, and they bring real income and food. They also destroy habitat, reduce crop yields and remove the raw materials that rural households depend on.

The study concludes that rural development and forest protection have to be planned together. It recommends regulated access to forest resources, stronger enforcement of forest laws, community education, replanting, and an environmental impact assessment before forest land is cleared for any project.

Key findings

What the survey found

  • 69%rated the pace of deforestation as rapid138 of 200 respondents
  • 160named economic motives as the main broad source of forest clearingof 200; demographic factors came next with 32
  • 79%said development pressure threatens the forest158 of 200 respondents
  • 47%named habitat destruction as the biggest effect on biodiversity94 of 200 respondents
  • 70%said food supply is the main benefit households draw from the forest140 of 200 respondents

Both hypotheses were supported. Chi-square tests at the 0.05 level showed significant differences between deforestation and livelihoods (χ² = 107.15) and between deforestation and biodiversity depletion (χ² = 98.60), against a critical value of 9.488.

Objectives and method

Objectives

  1. Examine the impact of deforestation on rural areas.
  2. Examine the socio-economic benefits and negative effects of deforestation in the study area.
  3. Determine the causes of deforestation in rural areas.
  4. Suggest alternative strategies of sustainable forest management for sustainable rural development.

How the study was done

  1. Study area. Ikwerre LGA, about 655 km², headquartered at Isiokpo.
  2. Sample. 200 household heads in Aluu, Isiokpo, Igwuruta, Ubima, Apani, Omerulu, Elele, Omademe, Omagwa and Ozuoha.
  3. Data. Questionnaires, interviews, participant observation and published literature.
  4. Analysis. Frequency tables, charts and chi-square tests.

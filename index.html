Featured Research: PhD Thesis

A Psychological Study of the Impact of Cultural Barriers on Pastoral Counseling Effectiveness in Buguma City, Nigeria

Ukwoma Obugheni Police · EIBCS2526P012 · October 2026
PhD in Psychology and Biblical Counseling, El-Elyon International Bible College and Seminary, Abuja

Setting: the 39 Polo of Buguma City, Asari-Toru LGA, Rivers State, a Kalabari cultural setting. Buguma is known for its riverine culture, Owuamapu water-deity worship, respect for elders, and the authority of the Amanyanabo and Council of Chiefs.

Method: a descriptive cross-sectional correlational survey of 407 pastors and adult church members across 51 PFN- and CAN-affiliated churches (53.1% female, 46.9% male). The sample size came from the Taro Yamane formula, and all 407 questionnaires were returned (100% return rate) through face-to-face administration. The instrument was the researcher-developed Cultural Barriers and Pastoral Counseling Effectiveness Questionnaire (CBPCEQ), with 38 items and a 5-point scale. The study is anchored on Multicultural Counseling Theory, African Psychology, Social Cognitive Theory and Stigma Theory, with biblical warrant for contextualization in 1 Corinthians 9:22.

Advertisement

Summary of findings

  • Respondents endorsed all four cultural barriers. Language differences (M = 4.12) and cultural stigma (M = 4.07) were most strongly endorsed, followed by preference for tradition (M = 3.63) and cultural beliefs and practices (M = 3.21). Prior research cited in the thesis (Wokoma and Briggs, 2024) links counseling in English rather than the heart language, Kalabari, to a 38 percent reduction in depth of disclosure among non-fluent English speakers.
  • Pastoral counseling was rated effective overall (M = 4.15). Strategies to mitigate the barriers received the highest agreement (M = 4.24), with training pastors in Kalabari language and culture ranked first (M = 4.29).
  • The five variables together significantly predicted perceived effectiveness, F(5, 401) = 10.95, p < .001, R² = .12, meaning they explain about 12 percent of the differences in how effective counseling was rated. Only language differences (β = .20) and strategies (β = .18) were significant independent predictors. The relationships were small and positive, the opposite of what theory predicts. The thesis attributes this to all respondents having already received counseling and to scores clustering at the agreement end of the scale.
  • The cultural beliefs and preference for tradition scales had low reliability, so findings on them are less secure. The study measured perceptions, not church attendance or clinical outcomes.

Recommendations

Train pastors in Kalabari language and cultural competence (Sue and Sue), run community education to reduce stigma (seeking counseling is wisdom, not weak faith), guarantee confidentiality with private spaces, involve elders as respectful partners (Clark; Nwoye), combine biblical principles with cultural understanding (Hiebert on contextualization), and build referral networks between pastoral and clinical services.

Most school leaders are busy and rarely have time to step back and look at the whole school. This short self-assessment helps you do that in about 20 minutes. It is not an inspection. It is a way to find where to begin.

## How to use it
Answer each question honestly with **2** (yes, regularly), **1** (sometimes) or **0** (not yet). If possible, ask two or three teachers to answer too, and compare. Differences often show where the real issues are.

## The ten questions
+ **Starting points:** At the start of each term, do we know where each child is in reading and maths?
+ **Teaching:** Do teachers present important lessons in more than one way (say it, show it, let learners do it)?
+ **Support for learners:** Do we have a simple way to notice children who struggle or have special needs, and to give them extra help?
+ **Classrooms:** Are seating, light, space and safety suitable for learning in every classroom?
+ **Teachers:** Do teachers receive regular training, feedback and encouragement?
+ **Parents:** Do we talk to parents about their child's progress at least once a term, and do we listen as well as speak?
+ **Safety:** Do we have clear rules to keep children safe, and a safe way for children and staff to report concerns?
+ **Records:** Do we keep simple, organised records of attendance, results and the support given?
+ **Using information:** Do we use results and feedback to decide what to change, not only to report?
+ **Welcome:** Does every child and family feel welcome, whatever their language, background, income or ability?

## Your score
Add up your points (maximum 20).
- **0 to 7: getting started.** Choose one or two questions to work on first. Do not try to fix everything.
- **8 to 14: building well.** You have good foundations. Focus on the questions where you scored 0.
- **15 to 20: strong.** Keep it up, and look at how you can share what works with other schools.

## Turn your score into a 90-day plan
+ Pick your **two lowest-scoring** questions.
+ For each one, write **one small action**, who will do it and by when. For example: "Head teacher and class teachers will list learners who struggle with reading in every class by the end of week 2."
+ Meet briefly every two weeks to check progress.
+ After 90 days, answer the ten questions again and compare.

## A few ideas for common low scores
- **Starting points:** a short oral reading check and a ten-question maths check per class at the start of term gives a useful picture.
- **Support for learners:** begin with seating, peer buddies and a one-page support plan for the child who needs it most.
- **Teachers:** a one-day workshop, plus a monthly 30-minute teacher meeting to share what is working, costs little.
- **Parents:** a short, positive message home each term works better than only calling parents when there is a problem.
- **Records:** one organised file per class is better than many scattered papers.

## When to get outside help
An outside view can save time, especially for assessing learning needs, training teachers or building an inclusive approach. Choose support that works with your teachers and uses examples from schools like yours.
